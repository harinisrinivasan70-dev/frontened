Indian temples are sacred dwelling places for deities and vital centers of spiritual, artistic, and community life. Built according to ancient geometric and cosmic principles, their architecture typically features a central inner sanctum called the garbhagriha—which houses the main idol—topped by towering spires or pyramidal structures known as shikharas in the north and vimanas in the south.

 Across the country, these monumental structures showcase extraordinary stone carvings and 
    diverse regional styles, such as the grand Dravidian complexes in Tamil Nadu and the intricate Nagara shrines of North India.
